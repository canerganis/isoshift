# Referee report, round 1

Manuscript: "A short-bond rule for the isotope effect on polymorph stability in hydrogen-bonded crystals" (docs/paper/main.tex, draft of 23 October 2026)
Target journal: Physical Review Materials. Secondary question: Nature Communications or npj Computational Materials level.
Referee: Claude Opus 5.5 (round 1). The review used the repository on branch post-hackathon; numbers were recomputed with `.venv/Scripts/python.exe -I` from results/decompose_pairs.csv, results/decompose_structures.csv, data/isoshift/pairs.csv, results/stretch_check.csv, results/hbond_geometry.csv, results/screen_structures.csv and results/screen_pairs_refined.csv, and checked against the JSON summaries and findings. Scripts live in the session scratchpad, not in the repository.

## 1. Summary of claims

The author computes harmonic H/D isotope free-energy double differences between polymorph pairs of carbon-free hydrogen compounds (220 pairs, 63 formulas) with three universal MLIPs (MACE, ORB, MatterSim), so that the static polymorph energy gap cancels. Across all clean covalent X-H rows, the 300 K double difference rises by 0.11 to 0.13 meV per H per mÅ of mean X-H length difference, and a Hellmann-Feynman mass integral splits this into a stretch term (about +0.25) about half cancelled by a bend and libration term. From this the paper derives a sign rule: when at least one polymorph has mean model O-H of 0.99 Å or more (a post hoc boundary), deuteration favours the polymorph with the shorter O-H bonds, in 185 of 212 model evaluations against 131 for a leave-one-formula-out constant-sign baseline; all 10 pair-majority failures involve one Al(OH)3 structure (mp-985293), and the rule fails for weak hydrogen bonds (2 of 20). A one-mode harmonic estimate gives a stretch slope of 0.32 to 0.33 against 0.25 to 0.31 fitted, the rule is shown to be a thermal rule (153, 185, 210 of 212 at 0, 300, 600 K), and a prospective test on new pairs fails its predeclared bar. The paper predicts that deuteration raises the diaspore to δ-AlOOH transition by about 1 GPa and proposes D/H partitioning between coexisting polymorphs as the direct test.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. The statistical unit is overstated and the headline count mostly measures a handful of oxyhydroxides.** The 212 evaluations come from 84 pairs built from only 89 unique structures in 28 formulas, and five formulas (AlOOH 39, FeOOH 31, Al(OH)3 28, MnOOH 16, CrOOH 15) contribute 129 of the 212. Pairs within a formula are combinatorial and close to transitive: if ΔΔF were additive per structure, the signs of (a,b), (b,c) and (a,c) would not be independent. One structure (mp-985293) produces all 10 wrong pairs. The paper says evaluations are not independent, but it still leads with "185 of 212". I ask for:
(a) a structure-level formulation. Regress the per-structure isotope term (F_D minus F_H per H) on mean O-H with formula fixed effects, which removes pair combinatorics and gives the slope its natural unit;
(b) formula-level counts in the abstract or main text. The rule is right by majority in 27 of 28 formulas (recomputed), which is a cleaner and still strong statement;
(c) formula-clustered uncertainty on the slopes. My formula-clustered bootstrap gives 95% intervals of 0.066 to 0.156 (MACE), 0.084 to 0.163 (ORB) and 0.089 to 0.169 (MatterSim). "0.11 to 0.13" is the model spread, not the uncertainty, and should not be read as one;
(d) robust regression. Theil-Sen gives 0.061 for MACE against OLS 0.112 (ORB 0.111, MatterSim 0.109). Al(OH)3 alone supplies 43 of the 188 MACE all-row points. The slope is sensitive to a few high-leverage pairs with abs(Δ) up to 100 mÅ, which are themselves the most anharmonic systems.

**M2. The baseline depends on orientation and is weak; give an orientation-invariant null and a competitor descriptor on the same rows.** Pairs are oriented with a as the lower database energy (src/isoshift_event/pairs.py), so the constant-sign baseline really means "deuteration destabilises the higher-DFT-energy polymorph" (62%). That is a physically meaningful baseline, but the paper never says so, and a constant sign is the minimum bar. The rule itself is invariant under swapping a and b; the baseline is not. I recomputed:
(a) a pair-level random sign-flip null (orientation-invariant): mean 106 of 212, p < 5×10⁻⁵ for 185. Report this;
(b) a density competitor ("deuteration destabilises the denser polymorph"): 104 of 212, chance. Report this, because it rules out the obvious "molar volume" alternative;
(c) the O⋯O sign on the same 211 headline rows: 183 right, almost the same as the 185 for O-H (Δx and ΔO⋯O correlate at r = -0.80). The paper's statement that the structure-only O⋯O rule "is weaker" (78% vs 61%) compares different populations with a different regime definition. On like-for-like rows the two descriptors are equivalent in sign. This matters: an O⋯O rule needs no MLIP relaxation and would be the practical result. Either show a like-for-like advantage for O-H or present the two as equivalent.

**M3. Harmonic physics is used exactly where it is least reliable, and no anharmonic or quantum control is run on even one headline pair.** The strong regime reaches O⋯O near 2.5 Å, where proton potentials flatten, the stretch is strongly anharmonic (harmonic minus fundamental gaps of hundreds of cm⁻¹, as the paper itself assumes in Sec. III D) and geometric (Ubbelohde) isotope effects change O⋯O by up to several hundredths of Å, comparable to the predictor's own scale. The paper evaluates D at the H geometry. Every failed or inconclusive test (F05 magnitudes 5 to 10 times too large, F07 ice wrong sign, the δ-AlOOH disorder window) points to this. With MLIPs already in hand, a referee will expect at least one of:
(a) PIMD thermodynamic integration over mass (the established route; Ceriotti and Markland 2013) for 3 to 5 headline pairs (diaspore/δ-AlOOH, boehmite/diaspore, one mp-985293 pair, one large-Δ pair);
(b) SSCHA or self-consistent phonons, or at least 1D anharmonic proton-potential corrections to the O-H stretch ZPE per donor;
(c) a quasi-harmonic or PIMD estimate of the deuteration-induced change of mean O-H, to show that x itself is stable under deuteration.
Without this, the paper's own limitation (1) amounts to "the method is not validated where the rule applies". At minimum, show whether the sign survives a stretch-only anharmonic correction of the size inferred from the F05 overestimate.

**M4. No higher-level reference for the quantity actually claimed.** The benchmark is per structure against PBEsol (a GGA, the same family as the training data), its per-structure 300 K MAE (0.5 to 1.1 meV/H) is comparable to the typical signal (median abs(ΔΔF) 2.4 meV/H on headline rows), and in the 2.50 to 2.80 Å stretch window the PBEsol comparison has one donor after deduplication (stretch_check_summary.json counts). The paper states that no DFT double difference was computed. Cells of at most 32 atoms make DFT phonons for 10 to 20 headline pairs affordable. I ask for DFT (PBE and preferably a functional with better H-bond energetics, e.g. SCAN+rVV10, r2SCAN-D or a hybrid on a subset) double differences for a stratified subset that includes the mp-985293 pairs, the largest-Δ pairs and the AlOOH pair. GGA overbinding of hydrogen bonds (the paper notes that ice XI is 7 to 9% too dense) moves both axes of Fig. 1 together, and only an independent electronic-structure reference can bound that shared bias.

**M5. The rule's skill is concentrated in rows far from zero and fails in pairs near a temperature crossover; say so and report accuracy against model error.** Recomputed: for abs(ΔΔF) ≥ 1 meV/H the rule is right in 151 of 159 rows (95%); below 1 meV/H it is right in 34 of 53 (64%), close to the constant-sign rate. The classical-limit flag separates the outcome almost perfectly: unflagged rows are right in 168 of 170, flagged rows in 17 of 42 (decision 08 already records this). Models disagree on the sign of ΔΔF in 19 of 84 headline pairs, and the median model spread per pair (1.26 meV/H) is about half the median signal. The honest statement is: "where the predicted effect exceeds the model spread or benchmark error, the rule is nearly always right; near balance it has no skill". That is more useful than a pooled 87% and changes how a user should apply the rule. Add accuracy as a function of abs(ΔΔF) normalised by model spread.

**M6. The prospective test undercuts transfer more than the abstract admits.** On the 24 clean prospective rows the rule is right in 16, the opposite constant sign in 19, and the pair majority is 5 right, 1 tie, 4 wrong (F11_prospective.json; the paper does not give the pair majority). The attrition (87 of 111 rows lost, mostly KDP-type and ice polymorphs with imaginary modes) also shows that the clean filter selects a population (ordered oxyhydroxides) on which the in-sample rule was built. The abstract's "missed its bar in a prospective test" should state the numbers. The paper should also discuss whether the strong in-sample performance is specific to the MO(OH) and M(OH)3 families. The leave-one-formula-out baseline does not address this, because the rule has no fitted parameter except the boundary.

**M7. The weak side is undersold and should be connected to the literature.** 2 of 20 is not "no skill": it is a consistent reversal, and 6 of 7 formulas are wrong by majority (only FeOOH, 2 rows, is right). The prospective weak side is 2 of 9. A sign change of the net quantum effect with hydrogen-bond strength is what Li, Walker and Michaelides (2011) and McKenzie et al. (2014) predict, which the introduction cites. The paper should either test the reversal (with the formula count as the unit) or at least say that the data favour a reversal at modest confidence and that this matches the known crossover. As it stands, the most physically interesting regime boundary is reported only as a failure.

**M8. The analytic estimate is partly circular and should be presented as a consistency check.** The softening s is fitted to the same MLIP phonons whose projected stretch term it then "predicts", and s_b is inferred from the observed compensation. Agreement within 4 to 34% therefore checks the projection and linearisation, not the physics, against an independent input. Either use an experimental dν/dr(O-H) (for example from Libowitzky combined with an O-H vs O⋯O correlation such as Steiner 2002 or the neutron data compiled there), or reword "places the rule within known physics" accordingly. Sec. III F also says the 0 to 300 K jump in counts is larger than bend weights give; with f changing only from 0.50 to 0.48 in the model, the estimate does not explain the 153 to 185 rise. Say that plainly in the conclusion as well, where "reproduces the sign and ... the size" currently reads stronger than the evidence.

**M9. The δ-AlOOH prediction needs a reality check against the known phase boundary and its pressure dependence.** The models place the AlOOH transition at 8.8, 13.0 and 15.1 GPa (4 to 6 GPa apart) without comparison to experiment (e.g. Ohtani et al. 2001 and the synthesis conditions of δ-AlOOH). The QHA isotope term at 10 to 20 GPa (G_D minus G_H difference about 2.3 meV/atom for MACE) is roughly three times the 0 GPa harmonic value (0.77 meV/atom). This is likely because the δ-phase H-bond strengthens towards symmetrisation, i.e. exactly where harmonic physics fails, yet the growth is not discussed. Report O-H, O⋯O and the isotope term against pressure for both phases, state whether any model symmetrises the δ-phase H bond inside the grid, and compare with Sano-Furukawa et al. 2008 (δ-AlOOD compressibility) as well as 2018. "About 1 GPa" in the abstract should carry "harmonic, outside the rule's validated domain" there, not only in Sec. III G.

**M10. The "direct experiment" needs a feasibility estimate.** Coexisting polymorphs exist only on the boundary, at high P and T, and D/H exchange between solids needs a fluid and high temperature, where α shrinks (the paper's own value of +359 per mil is at 300 K and high P). Give the predicted 1000 ln α at a realistic exchange temperature (e.g. 700 to 1000 K, at the transition pressure), compare it with SIMS or NanoSIMS D/H precision, and state the non-additivity term R or bound it. Otherwise the proposed test is not actionable.

**M11. Novelty against prior work should be stated more sharply, and partly conceded.** H4 (211 of 212) and "the shorter-O-H polymorph has the larger H force-constant trace in all 212 rows" show that at 600 K and above the rule reduces to Bigeleisen-Mayer and Urey: D concentrates where the H force constants are largest. At 300 K the rule is the zero-point-weighted version of the McKenzie stretch-versus-bend competition. What is new is (i) the quantitative net slope across polymorph pairs of inorganic solids and (ii) the room-temperature outcome and its failure modes. The paper says most of this; the abstract and Sec. I item (i) ("a correlation we did not find in earlier work") should say it too. The isotopomeric polymorphism literature cited is incomplete; pyridine (Crawford et al. 2009) is the best known case and is missing. The KDP Tc doubling (Ubbelohde effect) is the canonical solid-state isotope-dependent phase transition and is not cited, although KDP-type pairs dominate the prospective attrition.

**M12. Model independence.** Calling the three MLIPs "separate models rather than independent validations" is right, but the paper still pools their evaluations into one count and treats per-model counts as quasi-replicates. Since all three were trained on MPtrj/Alexandria PBE(+U) data, the "model" axis is closer to three fits of the same reference than to three methods. Report the pair-level count as the main number (74 of 84, with the model spread as a sensitivity) and drop the pooled 212 from the abstract, or justify pooling explicitly. The exclusion of SevenNet on a 15.3% versus 15% threshold for LiH TO(H) is also arbitrary when MACE is at +12.8% (tier_a.csv: 19.97 vs 17.7 THz). Either include SevenNet in the quantitative claims or explain why that cut separates the models in a physically meaningful way.

**M13. Process transparency is good but leaves a forking-paths problem.** The decision records disclose a great deal. Still, the choice of regime variable (r_max of mean O-H), the 5 mÅ eligibility, the O-H-pair definition (at least one structure with an O-H donor), the 300 K headline temperature and the "clean" criterion were all set after pre-freeze inspection. Nested CV covers only the threshold value. Recommend a multiverse table showing the count under the obvious alternatives (r_min, mean of the two, O⋯O regime, 3 and 10 mÅ eligibility, 250 and 350 K). Most of these are already in F01_cv.json and would cost one table.

## 4. Minor issues

1. Abstract and Sec. II D: "mean model O-H length" is computed in code as mean X-H over all H (decompose_structures.XH_mean_A). For all headline rows these are identical (I checked: no headline structure has non-O H), but the definition should say so, and the all-row slope mixes X = O, N, B, Si, P and metal-bonded H (27 non-O-H pairs). Report the all-row slope with and without non-O-H rows, or stratified by X.
2. Table I mixes percent (all-row compensation 54%) with ratios (strong-regime 0.52). Use one format. Add pair-level and formula-level counts to the table, and add the per-model formula-clustered CI for slopes.
3. Item 15 and 16 of Sec. 2: MatterSim mp-985293 compensation upper bound is 1.02 (1.0247), partner minimum volume is 7.53 (7.535). Item 25: "5 to 30% high" should be "4 to 34% high". Item 26: 7.7% rounds to 8%.
4. "Every reported number comes from three universal MLIPs" conflicts with the 572 pairs (569 for the three models) and with the brucite and LiH volume ranges, whose extreme values are SevenNet's. Either restate the ranges for the three models (brucite +0.19 to +0.33%, LiH +1.04 to +1.27%) or soften the sentence.
5. The MDR phonon database benchmark has no citation. refs.bib contains Petretto2018 and Torresi2025 but neither is cited in the text; remove or cite.
6. Eq. (1): state the normalisation used for every number (per H vs per atom of a common cell); several sections switch between meV/atom (AlOOH, F06) and meV per H without saying so.
7. Eq. (2) defines F_X with q weights but does not say that imaginary modes are dropped below 0.1 THz in the direct sum; this appears only in the clean criterion text. Move it to the equation.
8. Sec. II B: give the absolute quadrature error of the six-point Gauss-Legendre rule in ln m (a test against the direct difference with abs(ω) handling) and say whether the Hellmann-Feynman projection uses the eigenvectors at each intermediate mass (it does in the text; say it in the figure caption too).
9. Sec. II C: the Γ-free shifted mesh density should be stated (points per Å⁻¹ or mesh per structure), and the sensitivity of ΔΔF to mesh density reported, since the FeOOH card shows q-mesh sensitivity as large as the signal.
10. Sec. III B: "SevenNet gets 10 of 10 on the same population" is a different population (10 rows) from the 212; rephrase.
11. Sec. III D: the PBEsol diamonds in Fig. 3 are mostly outside the 2.50 to 2.80 Å window (one donor after deduplication inside it). State this, otherwise readers will take the diamonds as validation of f.
12. Sec. III E: "it is a thermal rule" is good; also give the 0 K count for unflagged rows (153 of 170), which shows that at 0 K the misses are again the crossover pairs.
13. Sec. IV, F04: the stretch comparator range (-511 to -11 cm⁻¹) makes the stretch sign untestable; say "sign not determined" rather than "as predicted" in Table II.
14. Sec. V: "A shorter O-H bond goes with a weaker hydrogen bond, so ... deuteration favours the polymorph whose hydrogen bonds are weaker". Connect this to the Ubbelohde direction (deuteration lengthens strong O⋯O bonds), which is the same chemistry seen from geometry.
15. Data availability: state the commit hash for the paper version, not only v1.0.0, since M03 and the post-freeze analyses are after the release.
16. AI-use statement: fine in substance; in Sec. II G reduce the operational detail (query counts, council procedure) and keep it in the statement or SI. PRMaterials readers will want physics in Methods.
17. Title: "rule" overstates a correlation that fails prospectively; consider "A bond-length trend in the harmonic isotope effect on polymorph stability ...".

## 5. Is the abstract accurate?

The numbers in the abstract all match the repository. The framing has four problems:

1. Overclaimed by unit. "185 of 212 model evaluations" leads, while the evaluations come from 89 structures in 28 formulas and five formulas supply 61% of them. "27 of 28 formulas by majority, 74 of 84 pairs" is the defensible lead.
2. Overclaimed by precision. "0.11 to 0.13" is the model spread; formula-clustered intervals span about 0.07 to 0.17, and the MACE robust slope is 0.06.
3. Undersold. The weak-hydrogen-bond side is a consistent reversal, not only a failure, and it matches the known sign change of the net quantum effect. The near-perfect accuracy where the signal exceeds model error (168 of 170 unflagged rows) is a stronger and more useful statement than the pooled 87%. The reduction to the force-constant trace at high T (211 of 212) is a clean result that the abstract omits.
4. Underspecified failures and predictions. "Missed its bar in a prospective test" should give 16 of 111 and the 16 of 24 against 19 of 24 on clean rows. "It predicts that deuteration raises the diaspore to δ-AlOOH transition by about 1 GPa" needs "in harmonic and quasi-harmonic calculations, outside the rule's validated domain" in the same sentence.

"A logged literature search found no cross-compound rule" is acceptable as worded, given the bounded search.

## 6. Recommendation

**Physical Review Materials: major revision.** The calculation is careful, the bookkeeping and disclosure are unusually good, the numbers reproduce from the repository, and the stretch versus bend and libration picture across polymorph pairs is a useful quantification. But the main claim rests on a post hoc regime, a weak orientation-dependent baseline, pooled non-independent evaluations dominated by a few oxyhydroxides, and harmonic MLIP physics with no anharmonic, quantum or higher-level electronic-structure control on even one headline pair, while the one prospective test failed. M1 to M5 are needed for publication. M3 and M4 (a small PIMD or SSCHA and DFT subset) are what would turn a plausible screen into a result.

**Nature Communications / npj Computational Materials:** not at that level now. The direction largely follows from Bigeleisen-Mayer and McKenzie-type arguments, transfer failed prospectively, and there is no experimental or beyond-harmonic confirmation. With an anharmonic control subset, a DFT reference subset and a positive measurement (for example matched AlOOH/AlOOD boundary runs), npj Computational Materials becomes realistic.
