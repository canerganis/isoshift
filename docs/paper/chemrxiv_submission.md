## Title
A short-bond rule for the isotope effect on polymorph stability in hydrogen-bonded crystals

## Author

Institution field: Independent researcher (Ankara, Türkiye). METU graduate is stated in the PDF.

## Abstract (plain text)
Deuteration can change which polymorph of a hydrogen-bonded crystal is stable. We formulate a rule for the direction and size of this effect and test it across compounds. To first order the H/D free-energy double difference per H between two polymorphs is ΔΔF ≈ S_str(1 − C)Δr̄, where Δr̄ is the difference in mean O-H length, S_str ≈ ½hc(1 − 1/ρ)s for O-H stretches at and below room temperature follows from the softening s of the O-H stretch with bond length, and C is the fraction of the stretch term cancelled by bend and libration. While C < 1, deuteration favours the polymorph with the shorter O-H bonds; where C(T) crosses unity the preference reverses at a crossover temperature. We computed ΔΔF, free of the static energy gap, in the harmonic approximation for 220 carbon-free polymorph pairs of hydrogen compounds with three universal machine-learned potentials. At 300 K ΔΔF rises by 0.11 to 0.13 meV per H per mÅ (formula-clustered intervals 0.07 to 0.17) with C about one half, and the one-mode stretch slope is 4 to 34% above the fitted one. Where one polymorph has mean O-H of 0.99 Å or more (a boundary chosen after inspecting the data), the rule is right in 74 of 84 pairs and 27 of 28 formulas by model majority (185 of 212 evaluations; constant sign 131); the 10 wrong pairs share one Al(OH)₃ structure in which bend and libration outweigh the stretch at room temperature. The rule is right in 123 of 124 evaluations where the effect exceeds the model spread, has little skill near balance and fails for weak hydrogen bonds (2 of 20 right). A prospective test on new pairs failed. Exploratory quasi-harmonic calculations put the AlOOD diaspore to δ transition at 300 K about 1 GPa above AlOOH. D/H partitioning between coexisting polymorphs would test the rule.
