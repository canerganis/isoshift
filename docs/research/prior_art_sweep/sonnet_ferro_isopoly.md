# Prior-art sweep: H-bonded ferroelectrics (geometric isotope effect) and isotopic polymorphism

Date: 2026-10-23. Question: could either literature pre-empt F01 ("deuteration favours the polymorph with the shorter O-H bonds when mean O-H >= 0.99 A, O...O <= ~2.9 A; fails for weak bonds")?

Method: web search, Crossref and OpenAlex abstracts, two PDFs read in part (Ichikawa 1987 PRB; Koval 2002 arXiv). Already cited in the repo and not re-litigated here: Ubbelohde and Gallagher 1955, Koval 2002, Ip 2012, Crawford 2009, McKenzie 2014/2015, Bourgeois 2018.

Reading depth is stated per paper. Full text of Ichikawa 1978 and 2000, Zhou 2004/2006 and Crawford 2009 was NOT read (paywalled or HTTP 403). Their statements below come from abstracts or search summaries.

## Literature 1: H-bonded ferroelectrics and the geometric isotope effect (GIE)

### What the literature actually states

1. Robertson and Ubbelohde 1939, Proc. R. Soc. A (I. The isotope effect, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1939.0028; II. Thermal expansion, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1939.0029). Abstract level: D substitution changes vibrational frequencies "without altering any other factor", so thermal expansion probes the crystal forces. The H-bond lengthening on deuteration (Ubbelohde effect) is established by this work and Ubbelohde and Gallagher 1955. Verdict: related. It is a within-structure geometry response, not a statement about which of two structures D stabilises.

2. Ichikawa 1978, Acta Cryst. B, "The O-H vs O...O distance correlation, the geometric isotope effect in OHO bonds, and its application to symmetric bonds", https://doi.org/10.1107/s0567740878007475 (volume and pages not verified; abstract not retrievable). Secondary summary (IAEA record): D expands O...O in the 2.44 to 2.64 A range, by up to 0.027 A; the asymmetric-bond correlation curve meets the symmetric line near 2.4 A. Verdict: related. It gives O-H against O...O and the sign of the GIE for a given bond. No polymorph-pair rule.

3. Ichikawa, Motida, Yamada 1987, Phys. Rev. B 36, 874, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.36.874 (text read). States Tc = 2290 (R - 2.428) K for tetragonal KDP-type crystals "irrespective of whether the crystals are deuterated or undeuterated" (R = O...O in A, about 2.40 to 2.55 A). Argues the large Tc shift on D comes from the H-bond length isotope effect (about 0.02 A), not tunneling, and that the isotope effect in dTc/dp is that of dR/dp. Verdict: related, and the closest precedent in spirit. It is a Tc(R) law inside one isostructural family, with D acting through lengthening R. It states nothing about polymorph stability, O-H lengths or ZPE.

4. Ichikawa 1981, Chem. Phys. Lett. (https://doi.org/10.1016/0009-2614(81)85040-3), title "Correlation between two isotope effects in hydrogen-bonded crystals: transition temperature and separation of two equilibrium sites" (title only). The 1987 text confirms an "empirical relation that connects delta-Tc and the intersite separation of a proton in the H bond". Verdict: related.

5. Ichikawa 1988 JPSJ 57, 2217 (CsH2PO4 type, https://doi.org/10.1143/jpsj.57.2217) and 1987 JPSJ 56, 3750 (squaric acid pressure and isotope effects, https://doi.org/10.1143/jpsj.56.3750). Same linear Tc(R) idea in other families. Verdict: related.

6. Ichikawa 1995, Ferroelectrics, "Geometric and quantum aspects of phase transition and isotope effect in hydrogen-bonded ferroelectrics and related materials", https://doi.org/10.1080/00150199508007861 (abstract read). States that the Tc isotope effect "has a correlation with the symmetry of H-bond and its geometric isotope effect"; linear Tc vs O...O among isomorphous members of the KDP, squaric acid, CsH2PO4, PbHPO4 and M3H(XO4)2 types; for M3H(XO4)2 the disappearance of the transition is a manifestation of the isotopic downward shift of Tc on H for D (D raises Tc, same sign as KDP), with zero-point motion the likely origin. Verdict: partly related. This is the most general statement in the family literature and the one reviewers will quote. It is still "within a family, Tc vs R".

7. Ichikawa 2000, J. Mol. Struct., "Hydrogen-bond geometry and its isotope effect in crystals with OHO bonds, revisited", https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-2860(00)00465-8. No abstract retrievable. Verdict: related, cite next to 1978. Not read.

8. Tanaka 1990, Phys. Rev. B 42, 10488, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.42.10488 (abstract read). Explains the GIE in KDP-type crystals (bonds lengthen on D) without tunneling, plus the accompanying O-H stretch and fluctuation effects. Verdict: related.

9. Koval, Kohanoff, Migoni, Tosatti 2002, PRL 89, 187602 (arXiv:cond-mat/0206029, text read). DFT: D depletes proton density at the bridge centre, weakening proton-mediated covalent bonding; the lattice expands, couples to proton off-centering and gives the giant Tc shift. Verdict: related. It supplies the mechanism for the sign (D favours the more off-centred, more asymmetric, longer-O...O state).

10. Wikfeldt and Michaelides 2014, J. Chem. Phys. 140, 041103, https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4862740. PIMD of squaric acid reproduces the >100 K Tc shift, attributed to collective proton transfer (tunneling) plus the GIE. Verdict: related.

11. Not found or unverified: "Ichikawa 2003" (nothing located); "Sugimoto and Ikeda" as a stand-alone paper (nearest hit: S. Ikeda et al., J. Korean Phys. Soc. 29 (1996), "Hydrogen motions in short hydrogen-bonds", abstract only: the asymmetric term of the H potential versus H-bond length explains the Ubbelohde effect across compounds). Do not cite either until retrieved. Horiuchi et al. (organic ferroelectrics, normal and inverse Tc shifts) is already in prior_art_recheck_2026-10-22.md.

### Does this literature state our rule?

No. It states four different things.
(a) D lengthens O...O in O-H...O bonds with R roughly below 2.65 A (Ubbelohde, Ichikawa 1978).
(b) Within an isostructural family Tc is linear in R, with the same line for H and D crystals (Ichikawa 1987, 1988, 1995).
(c) The Tc isotope shift correlates with the proton intersite separation (Ichikawa 1981).
(d) For ordering transitions D raises Tc, so it stabilises the ordered, more asymmetric state (follows from a to c and from Koval 2002).

It does not give: a statement across unrelated structure types or polymorph pairs; O-H length as the predictor; a ZPE split into stretch versus bend/libration with a slope in meV/H per mA; a weak-bond failure threshold; or a screen. The family studies concern order-disorder Tc, where the high-T phase is dynamically disordered and is not a harmonic polymorph.

### Sign consistency

Convention in the ferroelectric literature: D lengthens R(O...O), raises Tc, and stabilises the ordered phase, in which the proton sits on one O (shorter covalent O-H, longer H...O, a more asymmetric, less symmetrised bond). The paraelectric phase has H split over two sites, closer to a symmetric bond.

F01: D favours the polymorph with the shorter O-H, which is the weaker H-bond. For KDP the ordered phase has the shorter covalent O-H, so the signs agree when O-H length is the variable. In plain words both say D favours the weaker, more asymmetric, more localised-proton state. Consistent, not in tension.

Two points where a reviewer could see tension, to pre-empt:
1. If the variable is O...O instead of O-H. Across a family higher Tc goes with larger R, so D favours the larger-R member, again "weaker H-bond". Within one compound, however, the ordered phase of KDP has, as far as I recall, a slightly shorter O...O than the paraelectric phase. I did not verify this. If true, an O...O version of the rule would not map onto it, while the O-H version does. F01's separate O...O predictor (83%) deserves one hedge sentence.
2. Mechanism. Ubbelohde and Ichikawa are anharmonic: D narrows the proton distribution, changing the effective potential and hence R. F01 is harmonic at a fixed relaxed geometry per polymorph and compares ZPE. The sign agrees, the mechanism differs, and F01 omits the lattice response to D (the F02 wrong-sign oxyhydroxide volume cases are exactly this). Say so.

KDP, NaOH/NaOD and KOH/KOD are already declared negative controls in validation_set.csv, which is the right handling.

## Judgement

F01's contribution survives, but it has to be framed as quantification and boundary, not as the first statement of the direction. The family literature (Ichikawa and co-workers) gives a Tc(O...O) law inside isostructural families and shows D lengthens strong O-H...O bonds and stabilises the more localised, ordered state. Our sign agrees with that picture when the variable is O-H length, and our mechanism is different (harmonic ZPE at fixed geometry, stretch against bend/libration). The isotopic-polymorphism literature already says, for 4-MPPCP and in words, that D favours the polymorph with the weaker H-bond because the stronger, low-barrier bond has the smaller H/D ZPE difference (Zhou et al. 2004, 2006; Ip et al. 2012). ROY and the host-guest crystal are system-specific cases, and ROY is a weak-bond case with the opposite trend. None of them offers a cross-structure inorganic test, a slope against mean O-H, a stretch/bend split, a held-out pass rate against a baseline, or a regime boundary. The paper needs two sentences it may lack. One concedes that "deuteration favours the weaker-H-bond polymorph" was proposed for 4-MPPCP (Zhou 2004, 2006; Ip 2012) and fits the Ichikawa picture of D stabilising the ordered, more asymmetric state. The other says our rule is the harmonic, fixed-geometry counterpart, with the lattice response (Ubbelohde, Ichikawa) and tunneling left out, which is why KDP, NaOH and KOH are negative controls. Avoid wording like "new direction of the isotope effect" or "first rule linking H-bond strength to isotopic polymorphism".
