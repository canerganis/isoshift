## Graham, Sheppard and Heaton 1980 (GCA 44, 353), doi:10.1016/0016-7037(80)90143-x

**Triggers decision 15 "What would change this", item 2.**

* Abstract: "Fractionations are consistent with the general relationship: the shorter the O-H...O bridge, the more depleted is the mineral in D."
* Discussion: "within each mineral group there is a qualitative relationship between the length of the 'O-H...O' bridge and the fractionation: the shorter (and stronger) the 'O-H...O' bridge in the mineral the more strongly the mineral concentrates protium". The mineral groups are polymorph or near-polymorph sets: the AlO(OH) dimorphs boehmite and diaspore, and zoisite, clinozoisite and epidote.
* For epidote and AlO(OH) minerals "crystal structure is evidently the principal factor controlling hydrogen isotope fractionation".
* Boehmite is inferred to have a more linear, possibly near-symmetric O-H...O bridge than diaspore; the linear-bond members show "faster exchange and smaller fractionations".
* The relation is qualitative: no slope, no fit, no regime boundary, two mineral groups, T > 200 °C, bridge geometries inferred from IR, X-ray and neutron data.

Consequence: the sign of F01 at the polymorph level, in inorganic O-H...O minerals, was stated in 1980 from measurements. Stronger (shorter O...O) bridge means longer O-H, so "D avoids the stronger-bonded polymorph" is the same direction as "D favours the shorter O-H polymorph". Per decision 15, the option does not change: add "as Graham and co-workers noted for that pair" to the novelty sentence and to F12, and state the sign as prior art at polymorph level.

## Méheut, Lazzeri, Balan and Mauri 2010 (GCA 74, 3874), doi:10.1016/j.gca.2010.04.020 (review copy)

* Harmonic DFT β-factors for brucite, kaolinite, lizardite and gibbsite against water; systematic overestimate of 10 to 15 per mil, corrected by -14 per mil.
* **No plot or fit of ln β against ν(OH) or d(OH).** Decision 15 item 1 is not triggered.
* One qualitative remark (Sec. on internal fractionation): the inner hydroxyls of kaolinite are heavier and closer to gibbsite, those of lizardite lighter and closer to brucite, "likely related to the presence of hydrogen bonding in gibbsite and kaolinite and to its absence in brucite and lizardite". This is the weak-bond regime (no or weak H-bonds), where the H-bonded hydroxyl is D-enriched, the direction of F01's weak-side reversal. Different minerals, not polymorphs; it can be cited as consistent context, not as a test.

## Reynard and Caracas 2009 (Chem. Geol. 262, 159), doi:10.1016/j.chemgeo.2009.01.007

* Brucite only: DFT phonons, measured Raman/IR of Mg(OH)2 and Mg(OD)2, RPFR and brucite-water fractionation; sensitivity to OH stretch and low-frequency modes.
* No H-bond length correlation, no polymorph comparison. Nothing to change.

## Net effect on the paper

1. Novelty sentence: the qualitative polymorph-level sign is Graham's (1980) for boehmite/diaspore and the epidote group. What remains unpublished in what we read: the cross-compound fit, the slope, the stretch/bend decomposition, and the location in O-H length where the sign reverses.
2. F12 should say that Graham and co-workers attributed the boehmite-diaspore sign to the hydrogen-bond geometry.
3. Optional: Méheut's remark as weak-regime context next to the weak-side reversal.
4. The parenthesis "the full texts of Refs. Graham1980, Meheut2010, Reynard2009 were not accessible" must be removed.
