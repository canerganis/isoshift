Can one extra neutron change which crystal a material prefers?

Swap hydrogen for deuterium and the chemistry stays the same, but the atoms vibrate differently. In δ-AlOOH, a candidate carrier of water into the deep Earth, that swap moves a measured transition from 9.0 to 13.4 GPa. A logged literature search found no general rule for when this happens or in which direction.

At the OSI Hackathon 2026 we found one. Screening 220 polymorph pairs with three machine-learned potentials (MACE, ORB, MatterSim), we saw that deuterium favours the crystal with the shorter O-H bonds whenever hydrogen bonds are moderate to strong: 185 of 212 model evaluations, against 131 for a constant guess. The size of the effect follows bond length, 0.11 to 0.13 meV per H for each thousandth of an ångström, a correlation we did not find in earlier work. We call it the short-bond rule.

It makes a prediction a high-pressure lab can check: deuteration should raise the diaspore to δ-AlOOH transition by about 1 GPa.

As a metallurgy and materials engineer, I ran this with a team of AI agents built on models from @Anthropic, @OpenAI and @Google DeepMind. Claude Opus 5.5 ran the calculations, Claude Sonnet agents searched the literature with Bond from @Valency Systems Inc., and a council of Claude Opus, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon argued every claim before it went in. Predictions were committed before the data, and the tests that failed are published next to the ones that passed. Everything is open, including a @marimo app that runs on a laptop.
