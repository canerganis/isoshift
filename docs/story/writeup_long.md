# IsoShift: which polymorph does deuteration favour?

**Problem.** Neutron crystallographers deuterate samples to cut background; chemists use D2O or D2 to reach metastable phases. Both need to know when H to D moves the balance between polymorphs. The effect is a few meV per atom, below model static errors, so we compute only the isotope double difference, in which the static gap cancels. The organisers asked whether two days can reach a real discovery. Two days produced a testable, regime-limited computational rule, its failure boundary, one named counterexample and a falsifiable pressure prediction. It is not a confirmed discovery, because this observable has never been measured.

**Method.** Force constants are mass-independent, so one phonon calculation per structure gives harmonic and quasi-harmonic free energies for H and D. An ensemble of universal ML potentials (MACE, ORB, MatterSim; SevenNet in sign votes only) runs over Materials Project and JARVIS-DFT polymorph pairs. Measured volume isotope effects came first: brucite and LiH signs right in 4 of 4 models (Fig. 1).

**Result (F01, headline figure; Fig. 4 shows all rows).** Across 220 hydrogen-compound polymorph pairs screened with three universal ML potentials, the harmonic H/D free-energy difference at 300 K changes by 0.11 to 0.13 meV per H for each mÅ difference in mean X-H length, a stretch term about half cancelled by bend and libration. When at least one polymorph has a mean model O-H length of 0.99 Å or more (a model-derived boundary for moderate to strong hydrogen bonds, chosen after inspecting the data), deuteration favours the polymorph with the shorter O-H bonds in 185 of 212 O-H model evaluations with |Δ mean O-H| ≥ 5 mÅ (84 pairs, 28 formulas), against 131 of 212 for a constant-sign baseline fitted on leave-one-formula-out training folds. By model majority 74 pairs are right and 10 are wrong, and all 10 wrong pairs contain one Al(OH)3 structure, mp-985293.

The 0.99 Å boundary was chosen after inspecting the pre-freeze data and is kept for the frozen result. Re-choosing it inside each leave-one-formula-out training fold picks 1.00 Å in 30 of 31 folds and 0.99 Å only in the fold that holds out Al(OH)3, and gives 183 of 210 held-out evaluations right against a baseline of 131. In that Al(OH)3 fold the rule scores 8 of 28 against a baseline of 19, so a sharp, universal boundary is not established; the cross-validation covers the threshold value, not the choice of regime variable, grid or eligibility cuts. The 10 wrong pairs all contain mp-985293 (P2₁2₁2₁, listed by the Materials Project as experimental, no imaginary modes in any of the three models), whose mean model O-H of 0.996 to 0.999 Å sits just above the boundary while its mean O...O contacts are short (2.77 to 2.82 Å). In these pairs the stretch term has the predicted sign, but bend and libration cancel 95 to 132% of it instead of about half: MACE calls 0 of 10 right, ORB 0 of 9 and MatterSim 8 of 9, with every MatterSim value at |ΔΔF| ≤ 0.26 meV per H. Without any threshold the rule holds in 187 of 232 O-H evaluations (81%). Below the boundary it fails (2 of 20 evaluations, 13 pairs, 7 formulas). In the O-H strong regime the fitted total slope is 0.13 to 0.15 meV per H per mÅ, with bend and libration cancelling 50 to 56% of the stretch slope. A structure-only version using mean O...O ≤ 2.93 Å is weaker (187 of 241 evaluations with |ΔΔF| > 0.1 meV per H, 78%, against 61%). The evaluations share structures and models, the three potentials are separate models, not independent validations, and no polymorph isotope free energy has been measured to test the rule directly. SevenNet, used for sign votes only, gets 10 of 10 on the same population (baseline 7 of 10); as a fourth vote the count is 195 of 222 against 138, with 75 pairs right and the same 10 wrong.

**Prior art.** The stretch-bend competition behind the rule is established for hydrogen-bond isotope fractionation (McKenzie et al. 2014, 2015) and for competing quantum effects in hydrogen-bonded systems, and large-scale harmonic isotope thermodynamics has been reported for metal hydrides (Bourgeois et al. 2018). Du et al. ("Isotope Effects at Classical Cost through Mass-Differentiable Machine Learning", arXiv:2610.11325, 2026) learn path-integral isotope free energies (iso-EPIGS) for liquid water and one oxalic acid crystal, a method for single systems rather than a polymorph survey. A literature search (logged) found no earlier survey of H/D free-energy differences across polymorph pairs of the same compound. The contribution here is the systematic polymorph-stability version of that competition, its slope, and where it holds and fails in three models. See also Li, Walker and Michaelides (2011), Zhou, Kye and Harbison on isotopomeric polymorphism, and the Ubbelohde geometric isotope effect.

**Tests with predictions committed before the data were read.**
*F02, volume isotope effects.* Prediction-first volume-isotope comparisons show mixed transferability: alpha-AlH3 has the correct sign with a 1.3 to 1.5x overestimated magnitude; GaOOH and beta-CrOOH disagree in sign with references of limited comparability. Earlier brucite and LiH checks reproduce the measured sign, while ice shows a large quantitative error. The misses lie in the strong-H-bond regime, where harmonic and quasi-harmonic treatment omits Ubbelohde-type proton anharmonicity. These results do not identify a unique failure mechanism. F02 does not test F01: it passed where F01 has no skill and missed where F01 applies. α-CrOOH is not counted: H is disordered in the reference structure.
*F04, differential IR.* In one predeclared literature pair (goethite to ε-FeOOH) the O-H stretch falls and the assigned O-H bend rises, as predicted: nominal shifts are about -260 cm⁻¹ (from the midpoint of a 2600 to 3100 cm⁻¹ band, so anywhere from -511 to -11 cm⁻¹) against -273 predicted, and +290 against +195 cm⁻¹. The diaspore to δ-AlOOH pair was inconclusive. This is consistent with the sign of the compensating bend shift at the zone centre, not a measurement of its size or of the compensation fraction; the measured bend/stretch shift ratio (about 1.1) exceeds the model median (0.7).
*F05, mineral D/H fractionation.* All 6 scored signs and the order brucite > goethite > manganite are right in all three models; magnitudes are 5 to 10 times too large. Preset verdict: ordering only. The P04 scoring rule was committed together with its result (90ad4cd), not before it.
*Checks.* Fig. 5 (F03) compares model O-H stretches with measured spectra. F06: Codex reimplemented ddF_iso blind and matched ours to 1e-9 meV/atom on 9 of 9 rows; this checks code, not force fields.

**Cards (exploratory).** Exploratory harmonic/QHA prediction at 300 K: deuteration raises the diaspore to δ-AlOOH transition pressure by +1.13 GPa (ORB) and +1.33 GPa (MACE), using one linear fit through 10, 15 and 20 GPa for both isotopes (the MACE H root is extrapolated below 10 GPa), and by +0.3 to +0.4 GPa from 0 GPa geometries. The two models place the boundary 4.2 GPa apart but agree on the shift. The sign is the prediction; neither the size nor the sign is validated. δ-AlOOH undergoes isotope-dependent proton disorder in the same pressure window, which harmonic physics omits, and the boundary is crossed experimentally at high temperature, beyond the 300 K calculation. Goethite to ε-FeOOH, same status: +0.46 (MACE) and +0.40 GPa (ORB), roots extrapolated beyond the 3 to 9 GPa grid. Falsifier: side-by-side reversed-bracket runs on the H and D compounds under matched conditions. An opposite-sign shift contradicts the card; one not resolvable from zero is a null result.

**How the AI worked.** Valency Bond literature agents found the validation data; all 182 queries are logged (`logs/bond_queries.jsonl`). Single-pass extraction was unreliable (9 of 11 rows "ok", 2 survived checking), so a second reader and a board checked the numbers. Each formula passed a two-model mathcheck. Hard calls went to blind councils (Opus, Astra, Gemini) with decisions and dissent logged in `docs/decisions/`. F06 is a reimplementation written without our code.

## Post-freeze addendum (22 Oct), frozen F01 headline unchanged

After the compute freeze we ran four studies, each with its prediction or scoring rule committed before the result (decision 08; details in `docs/INDEX.md`). None changes the numbers above. *F08:* adding the O-H...O angle to the rule failed its predeclared SevenNet test. *F09:* the same 212 evaluations decomposed at 0, 300 and 600 K at fixed geometry score 153, 185 and 210. In all 28 rows with mp-985293 the stretch term keeps the predicted sign at every temperature, and the wrong total at 300 K comes from bend and libration (H2 passed); the crossover temperature is model-dependent (MatterSim mostly below 300 K, ORB 317 to 406 K, MACE 431 to 506 K). The predeclared zero-point weighting test failed (H1, 98 of 212), so we do not claim a mechanism for the crossover. At 2000 K the signs agree with the high-temperature trace limit in 211 of 212 rows after a disclosed fix to the pair orientation in the scoring script. 600 K describes the harmonic isotope term only; many of these hydroxides decompose below it. *F10:* a structure-only atlas of 1796 Materials Project O-H polymorph pairs; its blind literature test found no scorable measurement (0 of 26). *F12:* boehmite against diaspore, compared with mineral-water D/H fractionation from Graham et al. (1980) via a compilation: the sign agrees at 380 C in all three models, but the rule scores it inconclusive (no stated uncertainty, 25% exchange for diaspore). *P10, F11:* a prospective held-out test (111 primary rows, 42 pairs, 20 formulas) was committed before any phonon job (eb61ccf). F11 prospective test: FAIL against its predeclared bar; see docs/findings/F11-prospective-test.md. 16 of 111 rows were right; 87 were lost to the predeclared numerical rules (69 failed or unstable, 18 not clean), so the ceiling was 21.6% against the 75% bar, and on the 24 clean rows the rule (16 right) did not beat a constant guess (19). Transfer of F01 to new pairs is not shown.
