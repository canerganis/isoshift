## Decision

The working explanation: zero-point energy weights a force-constant change by about 1/sqrt(k), the high-temperature Bigeleisen-Mayer limit weights the H force-constant trace, so bend and libration (k about 5 eV/Å²) count about sqrt(45/5) ≈ 3 times more, relative to the stretch (k about 45 eV/Å²), at 0 K than at high T. F01 at 300 K is then the room-temperature remnant of the classical trace rule, and mp-985293 is the structure whose crossover temperature lies above 300 K. This turns the one named counterexample into a physical statement and a testable prediction (a crossover temperature), and it replaces the geometric explanation that F08 already tested and rejected. The re-scoring itself is post hoc; the mechanism is tested by predeclared hypotheses H1 to H4 (action 3) before any new calculation.

* mp-985293 crossovers on the stored grid: MACE and ORB between 300 and 600 K in all their rows; MatterSim between 0 and 300 K in 8 of 9 rows and between 300 and 600 K in the ninth (mp-985293__JVASP-29795).
* The 300 to 600 K gain sits entirely in the 42 headline rows flagged `classical_limit_violation`: 0/42 at 0 K, 17/42 at 300 K, 42/42 at 600 K. Unflagged rows are 153/170, 168/170, 168/170. The flag fires when |ddF(2000 K)| > |ddF(600 K)| (`refine.py` line 184), which a sign crossover between 0 and 600 K produces by construction, because 1/T decay needs hbar omega << kT and the O-H stretch has hbar omega/k ≈ 5000 K. Over all 1616 refined rows with these columns, 384 are flagged, 309 of them change sign between 0 and 2000 K, and the median |dd2000|/|dd600| is 1.79 flagged vs 0.46 unflagged. So the flag is mostly a crossover marker. It can also mark omitted imaginary modes, which is why H3 (product rule) is a gate, not a formality.
* The high-T direction is textbook: at high T, F_D - F_H tends to (hbar²/24kT)(1/m_D - 1/m_H) Tr K_H plus a mass constant that cancels between phases, so D favours the phase with the larger H force-constant trace, which the O-H stretch dominates and which shortens with the bond (Badger-type). That makes the 600 to 2000 K accuracy close to expected, not news. The news, and the part judges can use, is the competition at room temperature: where the zero-point weighting lets bend and libration override the stretch, and at what temperature each pair crosses. The 0 K accuracy (153 vs 145) must be stated plainly: F01 is a thermal rule.

**Why rank 1 beats the experimental test for first place.** Astra ranked boehmite-diaspore first and Opus round 1 did too; neither had the temperature data then (Opus found it in round 2, Astra did not comment on it). Argument, not vote: rank 1 is zero GPU, finishes today with near certainty, explains the only counterexample with a physical quantity instead of a fitted descriptor (which F08 showed does not work), and links F01 to Bigeleisen-Mayer theory that chemistry judges recognise. The experimental test has the higher ceiling but a low chance of yielding a defensible number (paywalled source, slow diaspore exchange at ≤ 450 °C per Graham's abstract, boehmite model MP-theoretical). Both run; the order decides who gets the conductor's attention first.

**M02, checked.** Line 59 is right: dP_iso [GPa] = -1.380649e-5 × n_H × T[K] × L[‰] / dV[Å³/f.u.] (k_B × 160.21766 / 1000).
